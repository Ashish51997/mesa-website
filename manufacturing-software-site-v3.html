The business grew. The spreadsheets didn't.

Every growing manufacturer hits the same wall. The order book is healthy, the machines are running — but the information holding it all together lives in a dozen disconnected places.

"What's our actual stock?"

Inventory lives in a register, an Excel sheet, and the storekeeper's head. The three never agree. You find out the truth during the annual audit — or when a dispatch gets delayed.

"Check the WhatsApp group."

Production status is a chain of photos and voice notes. By the time numbers reach a review meeting, they're two days old and nobody can verify them.

"The QC file is with Ramesh."

Quality inspections live on paper. When a customer complaint comes in, tracing which batch, which machine, which shift takes days — if it's possible at all.

"We'll confirm delivery by tomorrow."

Sales can't promise dates because they can't see production. Production can't plan because they can't see material.

None of this is a people problem. Your team is working hard — inside systems that were never designed to talk to each other.

One system. One version of the truth.

Imagine walking into Monday's review meeting and every number on the screen is live. An inquiry becomes a sales order, a production plan, a machine schedule, a QC record, and a dispatch note — in one flow, with nothing re-typed and nothing lost.

You see the plant in real time.

Production, quality, inventory, and dispatch on one screen — from the floor or from your phone.

Every product is traceable.

Batch to machine to shift to operator to raw-material lot. A complaint becomes a ten-minute lookup, not a ten-day investigation.

Decisions stop waiting for data.

Month-end reports become live dashboards. Gut-feel planning becomes plan-versus-actual.

The company

Mass Polymers is a mid-size plastic extrusion manufacturer. [REPLACE: two to three sentences on products, customers, and plant size — machines, shifts, headcount.] The order book had been growing steadily for years, and the plant had grown with it: more machines, more shifts, more people.

What hadn't grown was the way information moved. The systems that had comfortably run a smaller operation — registers, spreadsheets, phone calls — were now holding a much larger one together.

Life before

A production day started with a supervisor writing the plan into a register. Machine operators logged output on paper sheets at the end of each shift. Somebody photographed the sheets and posted them to a WhatsApp group. Somebody else typed selected numbers into Excel for the weekly review.

Quality inspections were recorded in a separate register kept by the QC in-charge. Stores maintained its own stock register, updated when time allowed. When a customer asked "where's my order?", the answer required two phone calls and a walk to the floor. When a complaint arrived, tracing the batch back through machine, shift, and raw-material lot meant paging through months of paper — days of work, sometimes with no clear answer at the end.

Nobody was careless. Everybody was busy. The information simply lived in too many places, in formats that couldn't be checked against each other.

The turning point

[REPLACE: the specific trigger — e.g. a delayed dispatch, an audit finding, or a customer complaint that took too long to trace.] Management knew the plant needed one system. What held them back was what holds most manufacturers back: stories of ERP projects that ran for a year, cost more than quoted, and ended with the team working around the software instead of in it.

The condition they set was simple: the system had to match how the plant already worked, and it had to prove itself in stages — working software early, not a big reveal at the end.

What we built

We spent the first weeks on the floor, mapping every workflow from inquiry to dispatch. Then we built a single connected platform, module by module:

01

Sales & inquiry

Every inquiry logged, quoted, and tracked in one place. Nothing lives in a personal inbox; nothing goes quiet without someone noticing.

02

Sales orders

A confirmed order carries its specs, quantities, and dates straight into planning. No re-typing, no version confusion.

03

Production planning

Orders become machine-wise, shift-wise plans against real capacity. Sales can finally see when a delivery date is realistic.

04

Machine logs

Operators log output, downtime, and rejections at the machine. The paper shift-sheet and the WhatsApp photo chain are gone.

05

Quality inspection

Incoming, in-process, and final inspections recorded digitally against the batch. Every result is attributable and searchable.

06

Inventory & stores

Live stock by item, lot, and location. Issues and returns update the numbers the moment they happen — not at month-end.

07

Material inward (GRN)

Every receipt checked against its purchase order, inspected, and lot-tagged at the gate — so traceability starts before production does.

08

Dispatch

Packing, loading, and dispatch documents generated from the order itself. What left the gate matches what was ordered and what was billed.

09

Customer complaints

Complaints logged against the exact batch and dispatch. The ten-day paper hunt became a ten-minute lookup.

10

CAPA

Corrective and preventive actions tracked to closure, linked to the complaint or inspection that raised them — audit-ready by default.

11

Dashboards & MIS

Production, quality, inventory, and dispatch on one live screen for management — on a desktop in the office or a phone on the road.

How we work

From factory walkthrough to live system — in stages, not one giant leap.

You should never sign for a year of work and hope. Here's exactly what happens, in what order, and what you hold in your hands at each stage.

1. Walk the floor

We start in your plant, not a conference room. We follow an order through its whole life — inquiry, planning, production, QC, stores, dispatch — and talk to the people who actually do the work: supervisors, operators, storekeepers, the QC in-charge. We map how work actually flows, including the workarounds nobody wrote down.

What you get: a shared, honest picture of how your operation runs today. Most owners tell us this alone was worth the visit.

2. Blueprint

Before any code, we put the whole system on paper. The Blueprint is a fixed document you can read, question, and approve:

  • Workflow maps — every process the system will cover, drawn as it will work, step by step.
  • Screen designs — the actual screens your team will use, so an operator can react to them before they're built.
  • Phase plan — which module ships first, second, third, and why.
  • Timeline and cost — per phase, fixed scope. No open-ended billing.
[REPLACE: redacted sample Blueprint pages]

3. Build the first module

The highest-pain module ships first — usually the one that came up most on the floor walk. Working software in weeks, used by your team on real orders. If we're wrong about something, we find out early, when it's cheap to fix.

4. Roll out in stages

Each phase goes live with training on the floor — not a manual sent by email. Supervisors and operators use the system on real work before the next phase starts. Each phase proves itself before the next begins, and you decide whether to continue at every stage.

5. Stay

Plants change: new machines, new products, new customers with new requirements. We support the system, fix what breaks, and extend it as you grow. The people who built it are the people who answer the phone.

Engagement model

Fixed-scope phases. Every phase has a defined scope, timeline, and price agreed in the Blueprint. You pay for outcomes, not hours.

Ongoing support. After go-live, a simple monthly arrangement covers support, fixes, and small refinements. Larger extensions get their own fixed-scope phase.

No lock-in. You own your data and your system. If we ever part ways, you keep everything — code, database, documentation.
